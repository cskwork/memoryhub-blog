How is your work helping your company, colleagues, or customers? Even small contributions matter. **Consider whether you've set your expectations for yourself too high.** If it's difficult to find meaning in the work itself, look for other values you gain through it. Financial compensation, stability, relationships, and self-development opportunities—you'll have values that matter to you.

Finally, don't place all the meaning of your life in your work. You can find fulfillment in diverse areas like hobbies, family, friends, and volunteering. Balance your work and life. By practicing these ways to counter negative thoughts and develop alternative perspectives, you'll find it helpful. While respecting your current mindset, try to find new meaning and move forward step by step without giving up.

---

## 13 Concrete Methods

1. **Break Down Goals**: Divide large tasks into small, specific short-term goals. Achieving them one by one will boost your confidence and motivation. Seeing your progress visually also helps.
2. **Positive Self-Talk**: Whenever negative thoughts arise, tell yourself, "I can do this," "I'm doing well enough." It's important to believe in and support yourself.
3. **Improve Your Work Environment**: Organize your desk and place items you like on it. Small visible changes can shift your mood and increase your motivation for work.
4. **Use Motivational Quotes**: Write down your motto or inspiring phrases and post them where you can see them well. They'll help you regain focus during difficult moments.
5. **Create a Reward System**: Decide on a reward to give yourself when you reach a goal. Rewards that create anticipation, like delicious food, shopping, or travel, become a driving force for work motivation.
6. **Actively Seek Feedback**: Ask your boss or colleagues about your strengths and areas for improvement. Constructive feedback becomes an opportunity for growth and a chance to reconfirm your value.
7. **Write a Gratitude Journal**: Every day, write down 3 things from your work that you're grateful for. Practicing focusing on the positive side is effective in reducing negative thoughts.
8. **Strive for 1% More**: Rather than pursuing perfection, try to do just 1% better than yesterday. Small practices accumulate into big changes. Develop a mindset of enjoying the process.
9. **Find Small Achievements**: During your workday, look for small successes or positive feedback. When finishing the day, always recall "3 things I did well today." This helps you discover your strengths and value.
10. **Relationships with Colleagues**: When you feel like you're working alone, interact more with colleagues. By solving problems together and encouraging each other, you can find meaning and motivation in your work. Good teamwork increases job satisfaction.
11. **Set Self-Development Goals**: Look for opportunities to grow through your work. Learn new skills and think about ways to leverage your strengths. You can find fulfillment in the process of improving yourself.
12. **Rest and Recharge**: When work stress accumulates, it's necessary to take some distance. Take a walk during lunch or engage in enjoyable hobbies to change your mood. Recharged energy helps you regain a positive mindset about your work.
13. **Check Your Values**: Reflect on what's really important to you. If your current work doesn't align with those values, you can consider what choices to make from a long-term perspective. Reinterpret your work as a process of moving toward your life's goals.

## How to Check Your Values

Reinterpreting your work as a process of moving toward your life's goals means reflecting on what your current work ultimately means to the life you want. You can try these specific methods:

1. **Clarify Your Life Goals**: Define specifically what kind of life you truly want and what dreams you want to achieve. Visualizing your goals by imagining what you'll be like 1 year, 5 years, and 10 years from now is also a good method.
2. **Connect Your Work to Your Goals**: Think about how your current work relates to your life goals. Look for even small connections. For example, you could think, "The communication skills I learn through this work will help me with the work I want to do later."
3. **Find Assets from Your Work**: Think about how the experience, knowledge, and relationships you gain through work can become assets for your future self. Even if it's difficult now, you'll discover yourself growing through your work.
4. **Small Actions Toward Your Goals**: While it's difficult to make big changes immediately, you can take small actions toward your life goals. You could study topics related to your goals outside of work or seek out necessary information. Those small practices will eventually lead to big changes.
5. **Positive Mindset**: Frequently ask yourself, "How can this work have a positive impact on my life?" Strive to find learning and insights in your work. The power of such a positive mindset is significant.

When the gap between your life goals and current work is large, you may feel frustrated. But don't give up. Your current work will certainly be a valuable experience in your life journey. As you continuously practice giving meaning to yourself, you'll soon feel a change in your mindset. I support your goals!

**10 Core Principles**

1. You cannot argue with undefined terms.
2. Separate claims from evidence—write them distinctly.
3. Correlation is not causation; enumerate alternative explanations first.
4. A single counterexample collapses generalization.
5. Examine the simplest possible explanation first.
6. Strong claims without numbers are weak.
7. Distinguish data, assumptions, reasoning, and conclusion in your records.
8. Specify exception conditions and scope of premises.
9. Calculate the cost of being wrong to check your conclusion's practicality.
10. I can be wrong; seek contradicting evidence first.

Small-scale Practice (Recommended 3-Minute Routine): Pick an article or assignment, check it against the 10 principles above, and rewrite the conclusion in 2–3 sentences.

**3 Simple Templates:**

- Proof by Contradiction: "If P then Q. Q is not true. Therefore, P is not true."
- Null-Alternative Hypothesis: "Set H0 and H1, then check if data can reject H0."
- Cause-Effect Diagram: "Possible cause → Intermediate variable → Result" (draw arrows).

|  |  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Cannot argue with undefined terms** | Define core vocabulary precisely first. | "Efficient design" → Define numerically (construction days, cost per m²). |
| 2 | **Separate claims from evidence** | Don't place feelings (claims) and facts (evidence) on the same line. | "This material is eco-friendly (claim). LCA carbon footprint is 25% lower (evidence)." |
| 3 | **Correlation ≠ Causation; find alternatives** | Just because things change together doesn't mean one causes the other. Find other reasons first. | Ice cream sales↑ and drowning↑ → "Warmer weather" is a third variable. |
| 4 | **One counterexample collapses generalization** | "Always/everyone/never" fails with just 1 exception. | "All students focus better at night" → Refuted by one morning person. |
| 5 | **Start with simplest explanation** | Check easy causes before complex hypotheses. | App is slow → Server status/network first, not rare OS bugs later. |
| 6 | **Claims without numbers are weak** | Without metrics or units, credibility approaches zero. | Instead of "greatly improved," say "processing time 3.2s→1.9s (-41%)." |
| 7 | **Distinguish Data-Assumption-Reasoning-Conclusion** | Separate "observed–assumed–inferred–concluded" into distinct lines. | Data: Absences ↑12% / Assumption: Cold season / Reasoning: Poor ventilation / Conclusion: Double ventilation frequency. |
| 8 | **Specify exception and scope** | State "for whom, when, where." | "Effective (for men 20s, 3x/week, 8 weeks, high-intensity standard)." |
| 9 | **Calculate cost of being wrong** | Be conservative on decisions where error costs more. | Gas leak alarm: False alarm cost < Non-alarm disaster → Set high sensitivity. |
| 10 | **I can be wrong; check contradicting evidence first** | Before confirming your conclusion, search for evidence that breaks it. | "Design A is cheaper" → First check hidden maintenance, shortened lifespan. |

### 2-Minute Practice Routine

1. One-line problem summary → 2) Define core terms (#1) → 3) Separate claim/evidence (#2,#6) → 4) Alternative explanations, exceptions, scope (#3,#8) → 5) Record DARC (#7) → 6) Check error cost (#9) → 7) Search opposing evidence (#10) → 8) Final conclusion + numeric support (1 line).

## Mini Case: "We should switch to Material A because it's eco-friendly"

- **Define (#1)**: Eco-friendly = LCA standard full-lifecycle CO₂e 20%↓ or more
- **Separate claim/evidence (#2,#6)**: Claim: Switch needed / Evidence: A vs. B = CO₂e 24%↓ (kgCO₂e/m²)
- **Correlation vs. causation (#3)**: "SNS popularity↑" is not a reason; actual LCA measurement is the source
- **Counterexample (#4)**: If durability↓ then replacement cycle↑ → Could actually increase CO₂e
- **Simplest explanation (#5)**: Current material installation error or waste management might be the issue
- **D→A→R→C (#7)**:
  - D: A's LCA -24%, unit price +8%
  - A: Maintenance cycle remains the same
  - R: 10-year TCO within +1.5% with CO₂e 18%↓
  - C: Pilot with A in common area (1 building) then reassess after 6 months
- **Scope (#8)**: Climate zone II, indoor finishing, 10-year maintenance standard
- **Error cost (#9)**: Wrong switch → cost↑, performance↓ → Try small pilot first
- **Opposing evidence (#10)**: A's potential VOC↑ → Conduct physical test first

**Conclusion:** "Pilot 1 building, if VOC and durability pass, expand broadly (10-year CO₂e 18%↓, TCO +≤1.5%)."

## One-Page Checklist (Copy & Paste)

- Defined core terms? (Include metrics/units)
- Separated claim/evidence? Added numbers?
- Distinguished correlation vs. causation? Found alternative explanations first?
- Any counterexample to the generalization?
- Excluded simpler explanations?
- Recorded as D→A→R→C?
- Specified exception and scope?
- Calculated cost of being wrong?
- Checked opposing evidence first?
- Summarized final conclusion in **1 sentence + numbers**?

## Top 5 Common Mistakes → How to Fix

1. **Lumping terms** → Start with one-line definition (#1)
2. **No numbers** → Force minimum 1 metric (%, seconds, currency) (#6)
3. **Mixing evidence and claims** → Separate into two lines (#2)
4. **Ignoring exceptions** → Mark subject, timeframe, conditions in parentheses (#8)
5. **Confirmation bias** → Collect opposing evidence first, then check (#10)

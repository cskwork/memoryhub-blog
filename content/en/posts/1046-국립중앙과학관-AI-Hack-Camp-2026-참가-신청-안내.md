This guide organizes the participation application methods, project proposal writing guidelines, participation pledges, and precautions for the AI Hack Camp 2026 hosted by the National Science Museum.

## Event Overview

The **AI Hack Camp 2026** hosted by the National Science Museum is a hackathon competition that discovers and realizes ideas utilizing AI technology.

### Participation Categories

Participants select one of the following four categories to submit their idea.

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| Digital Health | AI-based health/medical solutions |
| Digital Safety | AI-based safety management |
| Digital Inclusion | Social inclusion through AI |
| Digital Education | AI-driven educational innovation |

### Participation Format

- Team or individual participation allowed
- Maximum 4 team members can be registered
- For individual applications, write "Individual" in the team name field

## Participation Application Composition

Applicants must submit the following documents when registering:

1. **Participation Application Form** – Participation category, idea name, team name, applicant (team leader) and member information
2. **Project Proposal** (Form 1)
3. **Participation Pledge** (Form 3)

Save the filename as **[Team Name]AIHackCamp2026_ParticipationApplication** format and submit as PDF.

---

## Project Proposal Writing Guide

The project proposal should be written in up to **2 pages** appropriate to the selected category. (Font size 12pt, line spacing 160% or less)

### Idea Name

- Provide a concise name that reveals the core concept and direction of the idea.
- Expressions evoking policy, future changes, or citizen experience are recommended over specific technology names.

### Planning Background

- Problem definition
- Idea development motivation
- Written with consideration for the topic that explains the approach to solving the problem

### Idea Main Content

- Core structure and function of the idea
- Specific implementation strategy
- Step-by-step promotion plan

### Project Plan

- Explain the data, tools, and AI technology utilization methods for implementing the idea.
- Describe the technical implementation method and specific feasibility.
- Present a level that can be planned by the finals. (Example: idea proposal, prototype demonstration, etc.)

### Expected Results

If the idea is realized, describe expected outcomes from the following perspectives:

- Social impact
- Policy implications
- Changes in citizen awareness

## Participation Pledge

Participants must understand and agree to the following:

1. **Copyright of the submitted work belongs to the participant.** The host, organizing, and operating agencies can publish, post, and display materials for exhibition and promotional purposes.
2. Participants must ensure that the submitted work **does not infringe on third-party copyrights.** Any responsibility for copyright-related disputes rests with the participant.
3. If a winning work infringes on another's copyright or is selected through fraudulent means, **the award may be cancelled** even after selection, and the certificate and prize money must be returned.
4. Participants promise to respect other participants and organizers and not engage in **abusive language, discrimination, slander, or other acts that impede event operations.**
5. Participants acknowledge that they have reviewed the precautions and promise to faithfully comply with the contents.

Violations of the above may result in award cancellation or appropriate sanctions depending on the severity of the violation.

## Precautions

### Submission-Related

- Save the filename as **[Team Name]AIHackCamp2026_ParticipationApplication format as PDF** before submitting
- Multiple ideas can be submitted by the same participant, but selection/awards apply to **only 1 idea**
- Submitted documents must comply with the provided format and methods, and applications missing required items will be excluded from review
- **No additional modifications or resubmissions allowed after the submission deadline**

### Idea Requirements

- Must be an **original work** that has not been previously selected or presented at other competitions/hackathons
- Must not infringe on third-party rights such as copyright, image rights, personal information, or ownership
- If there are reference materials or quotations, **source citations are mandatory**

### Copyright and Deliverable Usage

- Results generated during the hackathon are considered **participant's creative work**
- Copyright of submitted ideas and deliverables (including winning works) **belongs to the participant**
- The host and organizing agencies can use (including modifications) deliverables for **non-profit/public purposes** such as event promotion, exhibition and content creation, result reporting, and archiving

### Review and Awards

- Review results are determined according to the evaluation criteria of the review committee, and **objections are not accepted**
- If identical or similar ideas are submitted, some ideas may be excluded based on submission order or review committee judgment
- Even after winning, if submitted content is found to be inaccurate or fraudulent, **award cancellation and recovery of awards**
- **Prize recipients are responsible for tax obligations** related to prize money in accordance with relevant laws

### Event Operations

- Participants must comply with event operations and safety management guidelines; violations may restrict participation
- If legal disputes arise from violations of the event guidelines, **all responsibility belongs to the participant**

### Disqualification Reasons

- If determined to be a winning work from another hackathon or plagiarism
- If another person's idea or deliverable is used without permission
- If AI usage is intentionally omitted or falsely reported
- If submitted materials lack participant identification information and cannot be verified
- If fairness is compromised in the hackathon operations or review process
- Repeated violations of the host/organizing agency's operational guidelines

- Why Burnout Happens

> "Do you know which occupation experiences burnout most in our society?"   
>   
>   "I'm not sure... Customer service industry?"   
>   
>   "No, it's full-time housewives. Do you know why?"   
>   
>   "Really? I'm not sure why. I understand moms work hard, but I don't know why they rank first..."   
>   
>   Do you know the answer? If you've noticed, you've already grasped the algorithm of burnout. The answer is that full-time housewives are 'the group with the least compensation relative to labor.' In other words, burnout isn't determined simply by how 'hard' you live.   
>   
>   Just as a loss of physical balance can damage your knee joints without excessive exercise, a loss of mental balance can lead to burnout without excessive work. That balance is the balance between labor and compensation. In other words, if the situation of not receiving compensation commensurate with your work lasts long, you gradually become exhausted even from daily life, and once you cross the threshold, burnout arrives. Here, compensation includes not just salary, but also psychological and emotional compensation such as recognition and sense of accomplishment from work.   
> <Micro Rituals>, Jae-Yeol Jang - Millie's Library

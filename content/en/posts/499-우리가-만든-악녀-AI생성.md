## 1.

Seo Jinhee has been different since birth. Her skin is unusually pale white, and her irises have a purplish tint. Doctors tried to explain this rare pigmentation disorder, but her appearance always drew stares. Whenever she walked, people whispered and stared, and when her photos were posted on SNS, comments called her monstrous.

As a third-year college student, Jinhee felt her existence was an inconvenience to the world. However, her life took a turning point in a cultural criticism class. Assistant Professor Kim Nara was amazed by Jinhee's sharp opinions and insights. For the first time, Jinhee experienced recognition for her "thinking" rather than her "difference."

"Your perspective is unique. That's a strength, not a weakness," Professor Kim said. Jinhee couldn't believe it.

From that moment, Jinhee abandoned her attempts to hide herself and joined the campus newspaper, starting to write. Her first article was about gender discrimination within the university. Her writing had a powerful impact, and many female students anonymously shared their experiences.

Lee Sua from the same department was a devoted reader of Jinhee's work. Sua was an admired student. Always top grades, over 50,000 Instagram followers, and student council president. She seemed to live a perfect life.

"Senior Seo Jinhee, you're amazing. How do you write so bravely?" one day Sua asked, visiting the newspaper office.

Jinhee felt her heart race, meeting someone who seemed similar to her. "I'm just... tired of hiding."

Afterward, the two often met at cafés to discuss social issues. Sua, who appeared perfect, harbored secrets too. She continuously packaged herself to meet public expectations, and that pressure was suffocating her.

"Everyone wants me to always be happy and perfect. But sometimes I just want to let everything go."

Jinhee found Sua's confession surprising. They lived in different worlds, but both were trapped in their own prisons.

## 2.

As their friendship deepened, Jinhee began investigating corruption at the university. She questioned where student tuition went and why scholarships concentrated on certain students.

"This could be dangerous," said Mingjoon, a newspaper colleague. "Professor Kim might even be involved. Be careful."

But Jinhee couldn't stop. The deeper she dug, the more shocking facts she discovered. Student tuition was being used for private interests of high-ranking university officials. Most shocking was that Professor Kim Nara, whom she respected, was at the center of it all.

"Jinhee, I heard about your article," Professor Kim called her. "Tell me what you found out."

Jinhee hesitated. "Professor... why are you involved in this?"

Professor Kim laughed. "The world is more complicated than you think. This is all for the school. Sometimes small sacrifices are necessary for a greater good."

"That's an excuse. Students pay tuition for better education, not to fill someone's pockets."

"So what will you do?" Professor Kim's voice turned cold. "If you write this article, you'll be destroyed too, not just me. Think about it—who would believe someone as strange as you?"

Jinhee was shocked. The person who first recognized her was now using her appearance as a weapon.

"Let's forget about this. If you want, you can work in my lab after graduation. We could do so much together."

Jinhee left. Her mind was confused. Should she do what she believes is right and be destroyed, or compromise and take the safe path?

## 3.

While Jinhee was deliberating, Sua called her excitedly.

"Jinhee! I passed the SBS student anchor audition! I start next week!"

"Really? Congratulations!" Jinhee was genuinely happy. Yet, she felt anxious. If Sua worked at that broadcast station, how would it affect her investigation?

That week, Jinhee finally decided. She wrote the article and attached evidence. Her newspaper colleagues were surprised but supported her. The article appeared in the campus newspaper and soon became national news.

The reaction was explosive. Students were furious, and the school promised an investigation. However, things soon took a strange turn.

An article titled "Professor Kim Nara Experiences Defamation from 'Monstrous Student'" appeared on a major portal site. The article emphasized Jinhee's appearance and questioned her mental state. Most shocking, one of the article's sources was Sua.

"Seo Jinhee always wanted attention. Her articles aren't truth but means to gain attention," the article quoted Sua as saying.

Jinhee was shattered by betrayal. The friend she trusted, the only person who listened to her story, stabbed her in the back.

That night, Sua called crying. "Jinhee, I never said that! They distorted my words. Please help me..."

"Why should I believe you?" Jinhee's voice was cold.

"Please... my first report at the broadcast station was this. I couldn't refuse. But they completely twisted my words."

Jinhee hung up. Now she couldn't trust anyone.

## 4.

The next day, "monster reporter" graffiti appeared throughout campus. Students whispered whenever they saw her, and memes mocking her spread on social media.

However, not everyone blamed her. Mingjoon and several newspaper friends stood by her side, and some students anonymously sent her messages of gratitude for her courage.

Despite this, Jinhee became increasingly isolated. Professors ignored her classroom participation, and she was always left alone in group projects.

One day, she heard that Sua would do a special report on university corruption. After hesitation, she decided to watch it.

"Today we present an in-depth report on university corruption," Sua's voice trembled but was resolute. "The recent controversial article by Seo Jinhee raised many suspicions. Our investigation confirmed that much of the content is factual."

Jinhee stared at the TV in surprise.

"We found evidence that Professor Kim Nara and several other professors misappropriated student tuition. More shocking, they coerced me into false testimony during this process."

The broadcast continued as Sua revealed how she was pressured, how she was forced to distort Jinhee's words. She looked directly at the camera and said:

"Ms. Seo Jinhee, you were right. I'm truly sorry for betraying you."

## 5.

After that broadcast, things changed rapidly. Professor Kim Nara and related officials were investigated, and public opinion about Jinhee gradually shifted.

Sua tried contacting Jinhee multiple times, but Jinhee didn't respond. She was still wounded by the betrayal.

On graduation day, Jinhee walked campus alone. She didn't know where to go. She had planned to attend graduate school, but after all this, it seemed impossible.

"Jinhee."

She heard Sua's voice behind her. Jinhee turned slowly.

"I won't ask for forgiveness. What I did is unforgivable." Tears welled in Sua's eyes. "But an opportunity came for you. MBC wants to make a documentary about your story. About the prejudice and discrimination you experienced, and your fight for truth."

Jinhee thought for a moment. "Why would they be interested in my story?"

"You have an important story. What you experienced speaks volumes about our society." Sua slowly extended her hand. "Let's do this together. Let's tell our story to the world."

Jinhee looked at Sua's hand. She was still angry, but she knew their story could only be complete if they did it together.

"I might have seemed like a villain. But that's just an image people created." Jinhee finally said.

"We can all play the villain in someone's story," Sua answered. "What matters is knowing who we are for ourselves."

Jinhee slowly took Sua's hand. She was ready to tell her story now. Even if it sounded wrong to some, it was her truth.

"Let's tell the real story of the villain we created," Jinhee said with a smile.

## 6.

The documentary "The Villain We Created" aired that autumn. It was a powerful critique of prejudice based on appearance, media distortion, and abuse of power. Jinhee and Sua honestly shared their experiences, and their story resonated with many.

After the broadcast, Jinhee received lecture requests from several human rights organizations. She decided to become a voice for those suffering from "difference" like her.

Sua left the broadcasting station to become an independent documentary producer. She decided to stop living to meet others' expectations.

The two occasionally met for coffee and talked. Their friendship wasn't completely healed, but they came to understand each other.

"Could we really have made different choices?" Sua asked one day.

Jinhee looked out the window. "I don't know. But what matters is we're here now. And we're no longer characters defined by others."

The two met each other's eyes and smiled. They had been called villains in their own ways, but now they were writing their own stories.

The view of Seoul outside the window still seemed harsh. People still judged by appearance, media still distorted truth. But somewhere, small changes were beginning.

Jinhee's skin was still pale, her eyes still purple. But now she wasn't ashamed of it. Her "difference" was her strength. And Sua no longer tried to appear perfect. She learned to acknowledge her flaws and mistakes.

As the two left the café, a few people recognized them and whispered. But now they didn't care.

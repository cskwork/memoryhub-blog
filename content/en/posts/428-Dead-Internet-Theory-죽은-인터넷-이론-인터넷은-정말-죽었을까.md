## 1. What is Dead Internet Theory?

Have you heard of Dead Internet Theory? It might sound complicated, but it's actually a very interesting concept!

According to this theory, the modern internet is no longer driven by humans; instead, it's mostly filled with content generated by AI and bots. In other words, most of the content, comments, and social media activities we see online are supposedly generated by automated systems rather than real people.

Think of it with a relatable analogy:

- It's like a once-busy shopping mall is now filled with humanoid robots.
- Though it appears vibrant on the surface, it's actually just machines moving according to their programmed patterns.

## 2. Why Did This Theory Emerge?

The Dead Internet Theory attempts to address the following issues:

1. **Content Overload**: There's so much content on the internet that its generation rate seems to exceed human capability.
2. **Degradation of Interaction Quality**: Online comments and discussions feel increasingly superficial and repetitive.
3. **Algorithmic Dominance**: The content we see is increasingly determined by algorithms.

## 3. Origins and Development of the Theory

Dead Internet Theory was first introduced in 2021 by a user named "IlluminatiPirate" on the "Agora Road's Macintosh Cafe" forum. This user claimed in a post titled "Dead Internet Theory: Most of the Internet is Fake" that the internet "died" around 2016-2017.

The theory consists of two main claims:

1. Organic human activity on the web has been replaced by bots and algorithms.
2. Government agencies or corporations are intentionally driving these changes to manipulate the population.

After The Atlantic published "Maybe You Missed It, but the Internet 'Died' Five Years Ago," the theory began receiving wider public attention. Particularly, with the popularization of generative AI models like ChatGPT, discussions about the plausibility of this theory have become more active.

## 4. Evidence Supporting the Theory

### 4.1 Bot Traffic Statistics

The most compelling evidence comes from statistics on internet traffic. According to the 2024 Bad Bot Report from cybersecurity company Imperva, nearly half (49.6%) of all internet traffic in 2023 came from bots. This represents a 2% increase from 2022 and is the highest level since Imperva began monitoring in 2013.

```
// Bot Traffic Increase Trend (2013-2023)
2013: ~30%
2018: ~40%
2022: ~47.6%
2023: ~49.6%
```

Even more alarming is that a significant portion of this is classified as 'malicious bots.' These engage in various activities including advertising fraud, account hijacking, and price information scraping.

### 4.2 Proliferation of AI-Generated Content

According to research released by AWS on June 5, 2024, 57.1% of all sentences on the web were generated through machine translation. Additionally, ongoing research by Originality.ai shows that 13.1% of websites host AI-generated content.

With the emergence of large language models like ChatGPT and Google's Gemini, it's becoming increasingly difficult to distinguish between human-written and AI-generated content. This is especially true as more websites utilize AI for search engine optimization (SEO).

### 4.3 Changes in Social Media

AI-generated content is particularly noticeable on social media platforms. In early 2024, AI-generated "Shrimp Jesus" images went viral on Facebook. These images received tens of thousands of likes and comments, with many comments appearing to be auto-generated by AI.

On Twitter (now X), numerous tweets starting with identical phrases were discovered. These tweets beginning with "I hate texting" received tens of thousands of likes, with many suspected of coming from bot accounts.

## 5. Dead Internet Theory from an Academic Perspective

Academic researchers acknowledge that some aspects of Dead Internet Theory reflect actual phenomena, while maintaining a cautious stance on accepting the theory wholesale.

Timothy Shoup of the Copenhagen Institute for Future Studies predicted in 2022 that "once GPT-3 is 'unleashed,' the internet will become completely unrecognizable." He projected that between 2025 and 2030, 99% to 99.9% of online content could be generated by AI.

Jake Renzella from the University of New South Wales and Vlada Rozova from the University of Melbourne explain that "Dead Internet Theory doesn't claim that all online interactions are with bots, but rather reminds us to be skeptical about public social media interactions."

Research published in Nature analyzed 14 million messages on Twitter over 2016-2017 and found that "social bots" played a disproportionately large role in spreading articles from low-credibility sources. This provides evidence that bots play a significant role in making content go viral on social media.

## 6. Real Case Study: The "Shrimp Jesus" Phenomenon

The "Shrimp Jesus" phenomenon is a perfect example to illustrate Dead Internet Theory. In early 2024, an AI-generated image combining Jesus and shrimp went viral on Facebook. Despite being posted without context or explanation, these images received tens of thousands of likes and comments.

What's more notable is that many comments consist of just a single word: "Amen." This suggests that a significant portion of these interactions may be automated by AI. Indeed, Meta (Facebook's parent company) introduced an option to provide AI-generated responses to group posts starting January 2024.

This phenomenon can be summarized in the following table:

| Characteristic | Human-Generated Content | AI-Generated "Shrimp Jesus" Phenomenon |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Context | Generally provides background or meaning | Image only without context |
| Quality | Varied (from expert to amateur) | Consistently high visual quality |
| Interaction | Diverse reactions and discussions | Monotonous and repetitive comments ("Amen") |
| Spread | Generally gradual | Rapid spread |

## 7. Is the Internet Really "Dead"?

While it's difficult to accept all the claims of Dead Internet Theory, it's undeniable that the nature of the internet is changing. So, is the internet really "dead"?

### 7.1 Exaggerated Aspects

Some claims of Dead Internet Theory, particularly the conspiratorial element that governments or corporations have taken over the internet for mass manipulation, are exaggerated. Nick Anstead, a social media expert at Royal Holloway, University of London, states: "While claims that most online content is fake are exaggerated, we should note that a significant portion of the content we encounter may not have been written by humans."

### 7.2 Genuine Concerns

However, the surge in AI-generated content and the increase in bot traffic are legitimate concerns. These can lead to:

1. **Degradation of Information Quality**: AI-generated content often lacks accuracy or remains superficial.
2. **Search Result Pollution**: Search engines may struggle to find useful information due to AI-generated content.
3. **Opinion Manipulation**: Bots can be used to promote specific views or products.
4. **Distortion of Social Media Experience**: Interactions between real users and bots change the nature of online communities.

### 7.3 Maintaining Human-centered Internet Spaces

Fortunately, not all of the internet is dominated by bots and AI. Human-centered spaces still exist:

- **Private Online Communities**: Platforms like Discord and WhatsApp maintain more closed and human-centered interactions.
- **Subscription-based Content**: Paid content is curated for specific audiences and doesn't rely on SEO.
- **Academic Databases**: Scholarly content undergoing rigorous peer review is still generated by humans.

## 8. Countermeasures and the Importance of Information Literacy

### 8.1 AI Content Detection Tools

⚠️ **Important Note!**
Identifying AI-generated content is becoming increasingly difficult. However, the following tools can be helpful:

1. **AI Content Detectors**: Tools like Originality.ai and GPTZero help identify AI-generated text.

   - These tools aren't perfect but are useful for evaluating suspicious content.
   - As technology advances, the accuracy of these tools will improve.
2. **Social Media Account Verification**:

   - Check account creation dates, posting patterns, and profile information.
   - Accounts with excessively perfect or consistent patterns are likely bots.

### 8.2 Critical Information Consumption

💡 **Pro Tips**

- Always maintain a skeptical attitude when encountering viral content on social media.
- Verify the same information from multiple trustworthy sources.
- Be particularly cautious of content that triggers emotional reactions. This is often a strategy used by bots to spread information.
- Recognize that even content appearing to be written by individual users might be generated by tools like ChatGPT.

### 8.3 Building Human Communities

- Consider participating in smaller, verified online communities.
- Rediscover the value of face-to-face interactions and offline engagement.
- Support high-quality information sources and independent media.

## 9. Conclusion

We've explored Dead Internet Theory in depth. While it's difficult to assert that this theory is entirely accurate, it's clear that the nature of the internet is changing.

The term "death of the internet" may be an exaggeration, but it should be taken as a warning that the human-centered internet is under threat. As generative AI technology advances, the boundary between human-generated and AI-generated content will become increasingly blurred.

What's important is not to respond passively to these changes, but to navigate the digital environment wisely through critical thinking and information literacy. Whether the internet is "dead" or not, we still have the choice of how to use and experience it.

## 1.

Dr. Tae-woo Kim's body was discovered in the basement server room of the National Institute of the Korean Language at 3:27 AM on May 8th. In the darkness, illuminated only by the blue glow of the computer monitor, his body lay slumped over the keyboard, stiff and cold. The monitor displayed text whose meaning couldn't be deciphered.

```
ㄱㄴㄷㄹ ㅁㅂㅅ ㅋㅌㅍㅎ
```

The police couldn't decrypt those cryptic letters made of only consonants. I, Jun-ho Lee, the director of the National Institute of the Korean Language, could roughly guess what it meant, but I couldn't speak of it yet. It was about 'roots'—a project that couldn't be shared with anyone, an innovative system that would redefine the future of the Korean language in the digital age.

"Director, what on earth happened here?"

In response to the detective's question, I stared at Tae-woo's face and answered.

"He... wasn't a mere researcher. He was a genius."

Tae-woo's death was ruled an accident. A heart attack caused by overwork and stress. That was the official announcement. But I knew better. This was just the beginning.

## 2.

Two days later, I met Seo-jin Kang. She was the most trusted AI linguist I knew and one of the core members of the 'Roots' project.

"I analyzed the code fragment found on Tae-woo's computer."

Seo-jin handed me a tablet. The screen displayed a complex algorithm diagram.

"Do you understand what this means?"

"The core algorithm of the project has been leaked."

Hearing Seo-jin's words, my heart sank. Five years of research, a bridge connecting the past and future of the Korean language, an innovative AI system that would allow Korean to evolve without falling behind other languages in the digital age—its core had been exposed.

"Can you trace where the leaked data went?"

"I tracked the IP address. It seems to be heading to Milbon Tech."

Milbon Tech. Korea's largest AI company. And its chairman, Min-suk Choi, was my former mentor from university days, someone who once shared the same dreams with me. Now he had become the biggest enemy of the 'Roots' project.

## 3.

At 2 PM, in my office at the National Institute of the Korean Language. Min-suk Choi came to visit. As always, he wore an impeccably tailored suit and walked with confident strides.

"Jun-ho, it's been a while."

Min-suk approached the window and gazed out at Seoul's skyline.

"I was curious about how your project is progressing."

"I'm not sure what you mean..."

"The 'Roots' project... no, the PURI project. Preservation & Utilization of Reimagined Intelligence. What a nice name."

He knew the exact name of my project. Security had been completely compromised.

"Is it right to conduct research funded by the government so secretly? Shouldn't it be transparent to the public?"

There was always rationality in Min-suk's words. That's what made him dangerous.

"Minister Choi, this project cannot be made public before completion. The risks of commercial misuse are too great."

"What do you mean by 'misuse'? A company using it? Generating profit? Why is that wrong?"

"This technology must be provided equally to everyone. Just as Hangeul was."

Min-suk burst into laughter.

"Do you fancy yourself King Sejong? But Jun-ho, do you remember who King Sejong's father was? King Taejong Yi Bang-won. The man who killed his brothers to become king. Like your father."

My father. As a high-ranking public official, he had been imprisoned ten years ago for bribery and abuse of power. That scandal had been both a stain on and driving force in my life.

"Minister, please leave now."

"Think about it, Jun-ho. We share the same goal—the advancement and preservation of the Korean language. The only difference is the means. You think the state is more efficient, while I believe the market is. That's all."

After he left, I looked at my reflection in the office window. Mid-forties, my shoulders, which had always been held straight, had somehow begun to stoop. I had spent a lifetime trying not to resemble my father, trying not to abuse power, yet I began to doubt whether what I was doing now was truly right.

## 4.

The next morning, news came of a second death. Researcher Ji-young Park, a linguistic expert in the 'Roots' project, had been found dead from apparent suicide at her residence. The same cryptic message appeared on her notebook screen.

```
ㄱㄴㄷㄹ ㅁㅂㅅ ㅋㅌㅍㅎ
```

Seo-jin and I analyzed the code in the server room.

"It's only consonants left. This looks like a cipher using the principles of Hunminjeongeum."

"Hunminjeongeum..."

The principles King Sejong used when he created Hangeul. Characters modeled after the cosmic principles of heaven, earth, and humanity, and the system of sounds. The most fundamental roots of Korean AI.

"Seo-jin, did you figure out what the code means?"

"Yes, Director. It's a warning."

Seo-jin showed me the tablet. The result of decrypting the cipher.

"'They're trying to pull out the roots'... This is the last message Tae-woo and Ji-young left."

Then my phone rang. There was no caller ID, but I knew who it was.

"Jun-ho, it's So-yul Lee. Can we talk? It's really important."

So-yul Lee. A graduate student I taught, who had shown interest in the 'Roots' project. Before I met with her, I told Seo-jin:

"Strengthen the server security as much as possible. And prepare backup data of the 'Roots' project. We may need to move faster than we anticipated."

## 5.

So-yul waited for me in a quiet corner of the university library. Unlike her usual bright and energetic demeanor, her face was pale and exhausted.

"Director, I found this."

She opened her notebook and showed me. The screen displayed some code from the 'Roots' project.

"How did this end up in your hands..."

"I found it accidentally while organizing the graduate school server. Someone seems to have uploaded it there. Out of curiosity, I analyzed what it was... and it seems incredible. Something that could change the paradigm of Korean AI..."

"So-yul, you haven't told anyone about this, right?"

"Not yet. But why must this revolutionary technology be kept secret? Everyone should know about..."

"It's dangerous. There are people targeting this project right now. Two researchers have already died."

So-yul's eyes widened.

"Died? The news said it was an accident..."

"The truth is different. This project isn't just a language AI. It's technology that could determine the future of the Korean language. It must be equally available to everyone, like Hangeul. It can't become a tool of capital and power."

During our conversation, movement was detected in a dark corner of the library. I instinctively grabbed So-yul's hand and helped her stand.

"Let's get out of here. Right now."

## 6.

That night, the National Institute of the Korean Language server was attacked by hackers. Fortunately, thanks to Seo-jin's preparation, the core data remained safe, but it was clear that someone was desperately targeting 'Roots'.

Seo-jin showed me the results of the log analysis.

"The attack originated from within the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism's internal network."

"An insider?"

"Yes, and this attack pattern appears to come from Director Ji-hoon Park's department."

Ji-hoon Park. Director of Digital Culture at the Ministry. My long-time rival and a junior of Min-suk's from university. The pieces were starting to come together.

Then my phone rang again. It was So-yul.

"Director, someone seems to be following me. It's been happening since yesterday..."

"Where are you right now?"

"Near Gangnam Station. I'm in a café..."

"Don't move. I'm coming right now."

I explained the situation to Seo-jin and hurried to Gangnam. When I arrived at the café, So-yul was nowhere to be found. Only her notebook sat alone on the table. The screen displayed that cipher again.

```
ㄱㄴㄷㄹ ㅁㅂㅅ ㅋㅌㅍㅎ
```

And an additional message.

```
ㅈㅊㅋㅌ ㅍㅎ ㄱㄴㄷㄹ
```

I immediately called Seo-jin.

"Another cipher. Decrypt it."

After a moment, Seo-jin answered.

"'Tonight in the Milbon Tech basement'... Director, this could be a trap."

"I know. But So-yul's in danger. Call the police."

"The police? If Park Ji-hoon is involved, we can't trust the police either."

"Then who can we trust?"

Hanging up, I looked out the café window at Seoul's night, filled with endless digital lights. Hangeul signs glowed in neon, and people walked about, absorbed in their smartphones. Our language had already penetrated deeply into the digital world. But its roots were growing weaker.

## 7.

Milbon Tech's headquarters was a 58-story glass tower in the heart of Gangnam. I entered the basement parking lot. A footstep echoed in the darkness.

"Director."

I heard So-yul's voice. She was hiding between parked cars.

"So-yul, are you okay? Who kidnapped you?"

"I wasn't kidnapped. I led you here. Myself."

Her voice had changed. Cold and resolute.

"What are you talking about?"

"Chairman Choi wants to meet you. I... I've been working for him."

Betrayal washed over me. So-yul continued:

"Director, the 'Roots' project is truly amazing. But what good is it buried in a lab? Chairman Choi can make this technology known to the whole world. Make Korean a global language."

"That's not it, So-yul. What Min-suk wants is monopoly. Control. He wants to integrate 'Roots' into his AI system and dominate the market. Then language becomes a commodity. Hangeul's spirit is equality and universality. Think about why King Sejong created Hangeul."

For a moment, I saw hesitation in So-yul's eyes. Then Ji-hoon Park emerged from the darkness.

"An inspiring speech, Director. But you must face reality. In the digital age, you can't survive on academic value alone."

Two more men appeared behind him. One was holding a gun.

"Kim Tae-woo and Park Ji-young... was it you..."

"I didn't get my hands dirty directly. But they knew too much. It was dangerous. Now you're the same."

Suddenly, all the lights in the parking lot went out. Complete darkness. A gunshot rang out.

## 8.

When I opened my eyes, I was lying in a hospital bed. Seo-jin sat beside me.

"You're alive. Thank goodness."

"Where's So-yul? And Park Ji-hoon?"

"So-yul is hurt but okay. Park Ji-hoon was arrested. So was Min-suk."

"What happened?"

"I followed you. I was suspicious of So-yul. The lights going out was my doing, and I had already contacted the police."

Seo-jin showed me the tablet. A news headline appeared.

"High-ranking government official and AI company chairman arrested for attempting to illegally steal state project"

"And there's this too."

Seo-jin showed me another article.

"Director of National Institute announces 'Roots' project... 'The digital future of the Korean language should belong to everyone'"

"When did I..."

"I made the announcement in your name, Director. Now it can't be undone. I thought the only way to protect the project was to make it public."

I thought for a moment. Seo-jin was right. 'Roots' was dangerous as long as it remained secret. Just as King Sejong had Hangeul proclaimed to all people immediately after creating it, this technology also had to be open to everyone.

"You did well, Seo-jin."

Seoul morning was dawning outside the window. Digital signs were turning off one by one, and morning sunlight began to illuminate the city. A new era of the Korean language was beginning.

## 9.

Six months later, in the Grand Hall of the National Institute of the Korean Language.

I stood at the podium and surveyed the audience. The hall was filled with researchers, students, and citizens. And in the front row sat So-yul. She had repented of her mistakes and rejoined the project.

"The 'Roots' project is now officially launching as PURI 1.0. This open-source AI system connects the past, present, and future of the Korean language. Just as Hangeul was a script for everyone, PURI will be technology for everyone."

An elderly man in the audience asked a question.

"In the digital age, can our language and writing survive? Won't we be overwhelmed by English and Chinese?"

"That's precisely the purpose of PURI. To protect the roots of our language while helping it adapt to new environments. Hangeul was an innovation by King Sejong 600 years ago. PURI will be the innovation of the 21st century."

As I left the hall, Seo-jin approached me.

"Director, do you think this is really the end?"

"No. This is just the beginning."

Together we walked out to the plaza in front of the National Institute of the Korean Language. There stood a new sculpture. A tree composed of digital network structures. Korean consonants and vowels were engraved on its roots, and modern digital language codes glowed on its branches.

Beneath the sculpture was an inscription in Korean.

"A tree with deep roots does not sway in the wind."

And beside it, another inscription.

"Code with deep roots will not shake the future."

Beep-beep-beep-beep-ding-ding-ding-screeeeech—

Two in the morning. The modem connection sound fills the room. While the entire world sleeps, I log into another world. A blue screen fills the monitor, and white letters appear one by one. The Hitel login screen. I enter my ID and password, and the familiar main menu appears.

```
[Welcome to Hitel]
1. Email   2. Communities   3. News/Info   4. Chat   5. Games/Entertainment
```

I press 2 and enter the literature community room. Scrolling through the member list, I spot an ID called "Starlight" and invite her to a private chat room.

```
[Solbreeze] Still awake at this hour?
[Starlight] Yes, I couldn't sleep tonight. Why are you online at this time?
[Solbreeze] I'm busy with work during the day, so I only have time for myself at night.
[Starlight] By the way, didn't you say you work at a venture company? The economy isn't doing well these days, are you okay?
```

"The economy isn't doing well." Starlight's words hit me hard. The economic news on the radio each morning has become increasingly gloomy. Ever since the Hanbo bankruptcy, I've been hearing news of consecutive bankruptcies among large corporations. Our company recently cancelled two web development projects. The manager's face grows darker each day.

```
[Solbreeze] We're doing okay for now. The internet is the future, after all.
[Starlight] The future... that's quite a reassuring word. You're always so optimistic.
[Solbreeze] Am I? Honestly, I'm anxious too. I just don't want to admit it.
[Starlight] How honest of you. That's why I like talking to you.
```

Our conversation lingers in my mind. We've never met in person. I don't know her face or real name. We only exchange our innermost thoughts through letters on a blue screen. Yet sometimes I find myself being more honest with her than with my family or coworkers.

---

"Junho, don't you think it's time to get serious? Are you really going to keep working at that company?"

Sunday lunch at my parents' house, my father's voice cuts across the dinner table. I've barely gotten a spoonful of rice into my mouth.

"Father, our company is doing fine. The internet will completely change how we live in the future. Even if things are tough right now..."

"Fine? What do you mean fine! The newspapers are full of company bankruptcies every day. A smart kid like you who could get into a big company like Samsung Electronics, and you're fooling around at some venture startup..."

My mother quietly grabs my father's arm. "Honey, not during meals..."

My father sighs. "I'm worried about you, that's why. You don't understand how the world works. You've been like this since college. You always chase after things that seem new and cool without thinking about reality."

Heavy silence descends on the dinner table. I try to swallow the rice in my mouth, but it won't go down.

"Hyung will find his own way to survive."

My younger brother Junseok speaks up. A third-year college student, he's busy preparing for job interviews lately. I'm grateful he's defending me, but I also feel bitter. Because I'm in this situation, my brother will probably seek out a more "stable path."

My father has worked for over 25 years as an accounting manager at a small company. College tuition, jeonse deposits, living expenses... he's covered everything with that "stable paycheck." To a man like him, my choice must seem like incomprehensible recklessness.

As I leave, my mother hands me a plastic bag. "I packed some kimchi and side dishes for you. Put them in the fridge and eat them."

My mother's hand holds mine for a long time. "Junho, don't overwork yourself. Your father only says these things because he wants you to succeed."

On the bus ride back, I look out the window. The signs on the street blur by. "Loans," "Startups," "Jeonse funds"... and "Rent for lease" signs everywhere.

The world was changing. Did my father understand that? Did I really understand it myself?

---

"Hey, did you hear? WizWeb shut down."

Youngmin approaches my desk with coffee. WizWeb was a web design company that started around the same time as ours.

"Really? I thought they said everything was fine just yesterday?"

"It was all bluster apparently. The boss borrowed money to pay his employees, but he couldn't hold on any longer. There's even a rumor that six months of salaries are unpaid."

I stare at the code on my monitor. The atmosphere in our company has felt strange lately too. New projects are dwindling, and the meetings between the boss and the manager keep getting longer.

"Hey, don't worry. Our company will be fine."

Youngmin pats my shoulder. But there's no confidence in his voice either.

At lunch, we head to a cheap rice bowl restaurant instead of the company cafeteria. Everyone at work is in frugal mode these days. Economic news plays on the restaurant TV.

"...As financial market anxiety continues, the won-dollar exchange rate has surged again. The government says it's preparing market stabilization measures, but..."

At the next table, middle-aged businessmen in suits are having a conversation.

"It's only a matter of time before the IMF comes. We need to prepare."

"You're right. Our company has rumors of restructuring starting this week."

IMF. International Monetary Fund. That word spreads among people like an ominous incantation.

On the way back to the office, Youngmin says, "Have you gone to any interviews?"

"No, why?"

"Just... to be prepared. I submitted my resume to two companies yesterday."

I have nothing to say. Interviews? A resume? I'd never thought about such things. I didn't want to acknowledge the possibility that this company could fail.

That night, when I logged onto Hitel, Starlight was already online.

```
[Solbreeze] What book are you reading today?
[Starlight] "The Song of the Sword" by Kim Hun. It's about General Yi Sun-sin during the Japanese invasions.
[Solbreeze] Quite fitting for these difficult times.
[Starlight] Yes. There's a passage that says, "Those who wield the sword do not see the sword's edge." It means that in a moment of crisis, you must focus on what you must do rather than your fear.
```

Starlight's words resonate with me. I've been trapped in fear lately.

```
[Solbreeze] Starlight, would you like to meet? In person?
```

My message appears on the screen. There's a moment of silence. My heart beats rapidly.

```
[Starlight] That's sudden. But... I'd like that. When should we meet?
```

---

Apgujeong Café "Blue Moon." I arrived 30 minutes early. Nervously sipping my coffee while checking the time. What kind of person is Starlight... or rather, Sujin? What does her voice sound like? Her laugh? Meeting someone in person after three months of only blue screen interaction creates a peculiar tension.

Then the café door opens and a woman walks in. With short bobbed hair and a black dress, she looks around and locks eyes with me.

"Sol... or, are you Junho Kim?"

I jump up from my seat. "Yes, that's right. Sujin Lee?"

We exchange awkward greetings and sit down. Our deep online conversations seem colorless as we start with light topics like the weather and the café's atmosphere.

"Do I look very different from what you imagined on PC communication?" Sujin asks.

"No, you're... brighter than I imagined from your letters."

"Is that so? You seem quieter than I expected. You seemed more outgoing online."

We enjoy discovering each other. Sujin works as a proofreader at a publishing company and dreams of becoming a novelist. When she talks, her hand gestures are lively and her eyes sparkle.

"Junho, to be honest, how's work going? You only ever said things were fine online."

I hesitate. "Honestly... I'm quite anxious. The company is getting into trouble. I didn't realize how fragile venture companies are."

"That's how everything is in the world. What appears on the surface is always different from reality."

"Are you satisfied with your job?"

She smiles bitterly. "Not satisfied, but I like working with words. It's just that publishing is struggling too these days. People aren't buying books."

Our conversation naturally turns to social issues. Economic crisis, youth unemployment, an uncertain future. Yet despite all these heavy topics, there's something special in the way Sujin sees things. She doesn't view the world only pessimistically. Even in crisis, she believes in human potential.

As we part near the subway station, Sujin says, "Junho, you don't need to only be honest behind a blue screen. You can be true to your feelings in reality too."

Her words touch my heart. Sometime along the way, I've separated the me in reality from the me online. Like two different personalities.

---

December 3, 1997. A day I'll never forget.

"South Korean government requests IMF bailout... agreement reached."

Employees gather in front of the TV in the company lobby. Everyone's expression hardens. This isn't just news. It's a signal that all our lives will change.

The manager calls an all-staff meeting. When I enter the conference room, the boss's expression tells me everything.

"Everyone, as you've probably seen in the news... we're facing a national crisis. Our company is no exception."

The boss's voice trembles. Three years into founding his company, he's poured everything into it.

"Unfortunately... we must reduce our workforce. We'll be cutting 30% of total personnel. The specific list will be announced this Friday."

As people leave the conference room, their expressions vary. Anger, anxiety, resignation. I look for Youngmin, but his desk is empty.

That evening, I call Youngmin. The ringing goes on for a long time.

"Hello?" Youngmin's voice sounds lower than usual.

"Where are you? I haven't seen you since this afternoon."

"I... went to an interview. I told you I submitted my resume."

Silence. I don't know what to say.

"Junho," Youngmin's voice comes again, "prepare yourself. We don't know who will be on Friday's list."

After hanging up, I look out the window. Seoul's night streets are still full of lights, but something feels different. The signs seem dimmer.

That night I logged onto PC communication, but Starlight wasn't online. Instead, the literature community bulletin board was filled with posts about the economic crisis. "What can literature do in times of crisis?" "The tragedy of Korean society in the '90s"... heavy titles.

The next morning, the subway on the way to work is quieter than usual. Anxiety is evident on people's faces. Every newspaper headline is about the IMF and restructuring.

As soon as I arrive at the office, a director calls me.

"Junho, do you have a moment?"

I follow him to the conference room. This director is the most senior manager in the company.

"I hear you majored in computer science and know quite a bit about network technology?"

"Yes, quite a bit."

The director is silent for a moment, then continues. "Actually, in this restructuring... we want to protect talent like you. But the company situation doesn't allow it."

My heart starts racing.

"I have a proposition. We need to cut about 1 or 2 more people from the development team. Could you give me your opinion on who is... well, who is least necessary?"

Suddenly my mind goes blank. Is he asking me to put my colleague on the layoff list?

"If you need time to think about it, let me know by tomorrow. But as you know, this is connected to your future too."

Leaving the conference room, my legs are shaky. I sit back at my desk but can't focus on work. The faces of Youngmin and my other colleagues flash through my mind.

During lunch, I'm sitting alone in the company cafeteria when my phone rings. It's my mother.

"Junho..." Her voice trembles, "Your father... he got laid off from work."

My heart breaks. My father, who came to work consistently for 25 years, his ordinary and honest daily life has crumbled in a single day.

"How is father?"

"He's just in his room. Can you come by tonight?"

---

I hesitate in front of my father's room. Knocking and entering, I find him sitting by the window looking outside.

"Father..."

When he turns around, his face looks much older than I remember. The confident expression he always had now shows deep fatigue.

"You're here. Have you eaten?"

His calm voice actually hurts my chest more.

"Yes... Father, this must be so hard."

My father is silent for a moment, then speaks. "I'm fine. Don't worry too much."

In the room are boxes with my father's company belongings. Twenty-five years of memories. Certificates, mementos, photographs...

"Junho," my father speaks again. "I think you should quit this company."

"What?"

"The internet or whatever... at times like this, stability is the best. Get into a big company while you have the chance."

I feel words of objection rising in my heart, but seeing my father's tired eyes, I can't speak them. His advice comes from 50 years of living life.

"I'll think about it, Father."

As I leave, my mother follows. "Junho, don't worry too much about what your father said. You have to walk your own path. But... it will be tough, so don't suffer alone. Talk to someone."

My mother's words move me. On the bus ride home, looking out the window, I think. The director's proposal, Youngmin's interviews, my father's layoff... everything is changing quickly.

---

Friday morning. I arrive at the company to find people gathered in the lobby. The HR department bulletin board displays the restructuring list.

I take a deep breath and approach the board. I read through the names one by one. My name isn't there. I let out a sigh of relief. In that moment, I spot a familiar name in the middle of the list.

Park Youngmin.

I run to the office, but Youngmin's desk is empty. His desk shows signs of packing. His desk clock, family photo, mug... all gone.

The director approaches me. "Have you made your decision?"

I look him straight in the eye. Something becomes clear in my chest.

"Director, I'm sorry. I'm not in a position to evaluate my colleague."

The director's eyes narrow. "I see... then you understand you could be next."

"I understand."

After the director leaves, I call Youngmin. Only the ringing sound.

That evening, I stay late at the office. In the empty workspace, only the computer monitor glows blue. Like a PC communication screen.

I log onto Hitel. Starlight is online.

```
[Solbreeze] Our company announced the restructuring list today. My close colleague got laid off.
[Starlight] That must be so painful. Have you tried contacting him?
[Solbreeze] I can't reach him. He's not answering his phone.
[Starlight] He probably needs time. Are you okay?
[Solbreeze] I don't know. I'm grateful I survived, but why is my heart so heavy...
[Starlight] That's human nature. Experiencing relief at survival while feeling guilty at the same time.
```

Starlight's words resonate with me. I'm torn between relief and guilt.

```
[Solbreeze] Actually... I have a more serious concern. The director asked for my opinion on which colleague should be laid off. Depending on what I said, someone's fate could have been decided.
[Starlight] That must have been overwhelming pressure. What did you do?
[Solbreeze] I refused. But I'm not sure if that was the right decision. Now I could be the next person cut.
[Starlight] No one knows for certain what's the right choice. But it seems like you acted according to your values. That has meaning in itself, doesn't it?
```

Starlight's words comfort me. As we talk, my heart gradually lightens.

```
[Solbreeze] My father was also laid off. A company he worked for 25 years...
[Starlight] That must be so difficult. How is he?
[Solbreeze] He pretends he's okay, but he seems deeply hurt. And he's telling me to quit my job.
[Starlight] From his perspective, he's giving advice based on his experience. What do you plan to do?
[Solbreeze] Honestly, I don't know. Whether I should stay at this company or find something more stable... I believe in the internet's future, but right now reality is too unstable.
[Starlight] You don't need to rush a decision. Maybe it's also important to be with your father through this difficult time right now.
```

Starlight's words calm my mind. I think I should go to my parents' house tonight and be with them.

```
[Solbreeze] Starlight, I want to talk with you more than when we met last time. When this situation gets better... could we meet again?
[Starlight] Of course. Anytime. Then we'll meet showing our honest selves in reality, not behind a blue screen.
```

I log off and leave the company. The cold December air hits my face. The streets are still decorated with Christmas ornaments, but people's steps seem heavy.

I take the bus to my parents' house. Looking out the window at Seoul's night streets, I think. I don't know when this crisis will end or what choices I should make ahead. But one thing is certain. The me who was only honest behind the blue screen is now starting to act according to my values in reality as well.

Standing before my parents' front door, I take a deep breath. I knock. Mother opens the door and looks at me with surprise.

"Junho, what brings you here?"

"I want to talk with father."

Entering the living room, father is watching TV. It's the economic news. He sees me and turns off the TV.

"Father, would you talk with me?"

I sit on the sofa and tell him about what happened in the company over the past few months, about today's restructuring, the director's proposal, and my decision. Father listens quietly.

"Father, the world has become much harder as you said. But... I want to walk the path I believe in. Even if I fail, it's my choice."

My father's eyes flicker. He's silent for a moment, then speaks.

"Junho... your father wanted you to walk a safe path. I pursued stability working at one company for 25 years, but that stability also turned out to be temporary. Perhaps... you might be right."

My father's hand grips my shoulder firmly. His eyes show both worry and understanding.

"Walk your own path. Just remember you're not alone."

That night, lying in my old room at my parents' house, I look at the stars outside the window. Starlight... Sujin comes to mind. Just as she used the ID "Starlight" online, I was hiding under the name "Solbreeze." But now it's different.

The real me who existed only beyond the blue screen is now stepping into reality. The uncertain future frightens me, but I'm learning to walk alongside that fear.

The stars outside the window sparkle. Like Starlight in PC communication.

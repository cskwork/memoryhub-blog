## 1. Spring Rain

It's raining. On my first semester opening day after military discharge, my neatly pressed shirt and slacks, like a school uniform, are getting soaked. I left my umbrella in the dorm. Five minutes before class starts, my shoulders grow heavier as I rush to the Modern Literature and Technology lecture room.

"Damn it."

Catching my breath, I open the classroom door. Most seats are taken, with only one empty spot in the back row by the window. Next to it sits a female student with long straight hair, her gaze fixed outside. I carefully sit down. As I fold my wet umbrella, water droplets fall on her laptop.

"Oh, I'm sorry."

Flustered, I pull out a handkerchief to wipe it, but she quickly pulls out tissue and wipes it herself.

"It's fine. You got rained on a lot."

Her eyes are smiling. Her black eyes sparkle transparently. Outside, the rain intensifies.

"Junhyuk, right? I heard during introductions. I'm Yumi Yoon."

She points to her name tag. I nod and she smiles brighter. The professor enters and class begins.

"This semester's project will be team assignments with the theme 'Fusion of Literature and Technology.' I'll divide teams by the last digits of your student IDs."

Fate, perhaps. Our student ID endings matched, so Yumi and I ended up in the same team. Classes featuring novels I read during military service have sparked my interest in literature, and from that day on, I look forward to coming.

## 2. Cherry Blossoms

"Junhyuk, why does an engineering student like you take literature class?"

Yumi asks this while we sit on a bench discussing our team assignment, cherry blossoms drifting around us. Her hair flutters in the spring breeze.

"I had lots of free time in the military. With nothing to read, I read many novels. I especially like author Yi Sang."

"Wow, that's unexpected. You're really an engineering student?"

Yumi laughs playfully. Her laptop has a sticker that reads "Someday a Writer."

"Yumi, you dream of becoming a writer?"

"More like a hope than a dream... But my parents want me to become a publisher's editor."

A brief silence. A cherry blossom lands on Yumi's hair. I naturally reach out to remove it. Our hands brush, and a strange tension lingers in the air.

"For our project, should we go with 'Changes in Novel Form in the Digital Age'? I think you could handle the literary analysis and I could handle the technological aspects."

Yumi's voice sounds slightly higher than usual as she tries to concentrate on the assignment.

## 3. Library Nights

"Junhyuk, what do you think of this? How social media has impacted the narrative structure of contemporary novels?"

A week before midterms, we're cramming late in a secluded library corner. At 2 AM, it's pitch black outside. Yumi's face glows pale in the laptop light.

"The fragmented information delivery of SNS seems to influence the non-linear structure of modern novels. Looking at this paper..."

Throughout my explanation, Yumi nods and takes notes. The sight of her concentrating is beautiful. Is this what the campus romance I dreamed of during military service was supposed to look like?

"Junhyuk, want some coffee? Vending machine coffee?"

"Let's go together."

We walk through an empty hallway in the middle of the night. Our shadows stretch side by side under fluorescent lights.

"Um... Are you free this Saturday? There's a small reading at the café where I work."

Yumi asks this with lowered eyes in front of the vending machine.

"A reading?"

"Yes, I'm planning to read a short story I wrote. If you come... I think it would give me strength."

Yumi's cheeks flush as she finishes speaking. I pull out a coffee and hand it to her.

"I'll definitely go."

We both stand awkwardly holding coffee cups, unable to meet each other's eyes.

---

## 4. Books and Coffee

I enter "Books and Coffee," a small independent bookstore-café where Yumi works. Between books densely packed on wooden shelves, Yumi stands in a white dress. She spots me and smiles brightly.

"Thanks for coming."

The reading begins. Yumi reads her short story "Spring by the Window"—about a young man who finds comfort reading books while serving in the military, and a woman who sends him letters. It's strangely similar to us.

"Could this be... about me?"

The reading ends and we go to Hangang Park. Early summer evening, sunset colors reflect on the river.

"Maybe I was inspired."

Yumi smiles shyly. At that moment, raindrops begin falling from the sky. I open an umbrella. Yumi naturally moves closer to my side. Our shoulders touch under the narrow umbrella.

"What did you do on rainy days in the military?"

"I'd look out the window and imagine my life after discharge. Maybe I dreamed of moments like this."

My heart races. Gathering courage, I take her hand. Yumi doesn't pull away. We walk silently along the Han River, occasionally glancing at each other, bursting into laughter when our eyes meet.

"Yumi..."

"Yes?"

"Are we... dating?"

"Did you just confess?"

She asks playfully.

"Yes, that's right. Yumi, will you date me?"

"I already know the answer."

She leans her head on my shoulder. At that moment, the rain stops. I fold the umbrella, but we stay close together.

## 5. End of Summer

"Yumi, why don't you contact me much lately? Are you busy with assignments?"

I send a KakaoTalk message and wait for a reply. August, as summer vacation ends—me busy with job preparation and Yumi preparing for a short story contest—we naturally meet less frequently.

"Sorry. The contest deadline is tomorrow so I'm swamped. I'll contact you when it's over."

A brief reply comes. Late at night, thinking of her, I write my cover letter in the library. Outside, summer rain pours down. Like our first meeting. I want to bring her an umbrella, but I don't want to disturb her.

The next day is Yumi's contest deadline. Worried by her silence, I go to the café where she works. There, Yumi is talking with another male student. They look intimate. I notice how Yumi laughs—it's the expression she wears when looking at me.

I turn around and leave. Suddenly, I remember receiving a breakup text from my ex-girlfriend during military service: "I'm seeing someone else." My chest feels heavy.

## 6. Misunderstanding

"Junhyuk, why aren't you responding suddenly? Is something wrong?"

Yumi's KakaoTalk messages pile up unread all night. I don't reply. Preoccupied with job preparation and rejection notifications, the image of Yumi with another man keeps replaying.

An Instagram photo catches my eye. A photo of Yumi and the man I saw at the café together. The comment reads "Senior Hyunwoo, thanks for the advice!" Senior? My heart still feels complicated.

Eventually, Yumi finds me at my officetel. When I open the door, Yumi stands there soaked from rain, seeming to have rushed without an umbrella.

"Why didn't you answer? I was worried!"

"I was busy. Job preparation."

"Liar. Since when did you misunderstand after seeing photos I posted on Instagram?"

Her sharp intuition makes me speechless.

"Hyunwoo Senior is a literature club senior. He just gave advice because he knows one of the contest judges. How could you..."

"I know. I was just... anxious. Afraid you'd meet someone more suitable."

"Don't say foolish things. I want you."

Yumi's voice trembles. Suddenly, tears fall.

"The contest results... I didn't win. No one seems interested in my story."

I hold her tight. Outside, rain still falls.

"I'm sorry. I should have been by your side."

"Junhyuk, let's be honest. You're anxious too, right? Jobs, the future... me too. I'm anxious about becoming a writer."

Yumi speaks in a tired voice. We sit facing each other and talk all night. Anxiety about jobs, worries about dreams, fears about relationships. Speaking them aloud makes the fear diminish a bit.

## 7. Winter Gift

In December when the first snow falls, it's Yumi's birthday. I wait for her in front of Hankuk University's main gate. A small gift box sits nervously in my pocket as I check the time.

"Were you waiting long?"

Yumi runs over with a white scarf. Her nose tip is red from the cold.

"No, I just arrived."

Holding hands, we head to the prepared restaurant. After dinner, we go to the Han River. The river glows with city lights reflected on its surface.

"Birthday present."

I hand her the small box. Yumi opens it carefully. It's a blank notebook. On the first page, written in my handwriting:

"Waiting for Yumi's stories. Your Reader #1, Seo Junhyuk."

"Junhyuk..."

Tears well in Yumi's eyes.

"I've read all your stories. Your narrative has meaning. Someday it will definitely shine."

"Thank you..."

Yumi pulls an envelope from her bag.

"I have a gift too."

It's a letter—a short story Yumi wrote. The title is "Under Your Umbrella." About two people who meet by chance on a rainy day. Our story.

"I haven't written the ending yet. Since it's our story, I want to write the ending together."

Snow begins to fall. I pull Yumi into my arms without thinking. Her warmth transfers to me.

"Yumi, I hope you don't give up your dream of becoming a writer. I'll find my own path too. Even if it's uncertain now..."

"Let's go together. Walk our own paths, but holding each other's hands."

Yumi whispers near my ear. Just as she says, we carry our own anxieties, but we'll hold each other's hands and walk together under the same umbrella. In the December snow, we promise a new beginning.

---

## 8. Spring Again

Early March, a week before the new semester, cherry blossoms begin to bloom. As I prepare for both job hunting and graduate school, and Yumi decides to pursue graduate school in creative writing, we sit on campus watching cherry blossoms.

"A year ago at this time, we first met, right?"

Yumi asks.

"Yeah, it was a rainy day."

"Remember? You dropped water on my laptop back then."

"How do you remember such things?"

"Because every moment is precious."

Yumi laughs. Her laptop still has the "Someday a Writer" sticker. But next to it now is a new sticker I gave her: "Already a Writer."

"Yumi, how's your new novel coming?"

"I need to write more. This one is different. A story about young people carrying fear about an uncertain future."

"Sounds familiar..."

We look at each other and laugh. Then raindrops begin falling from the sky. I open the umbrella I prepared.

"You brought an umbrella?"

"From now on, we're under the same umbrella."

Yumi leans on my shoulder. We walk across campus in the rain. An uncertain future still awaits us, but together, it doesn't feel frightening. Supporting each other's dreams while walking our own paths under the same umbrella.

The rain stops and sunlight begins to shine. New semester, new beginning. Our story continues.

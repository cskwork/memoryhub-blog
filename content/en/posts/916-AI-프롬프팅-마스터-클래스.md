## 1. Overview: The Essence of Prompting

Most people are disappointed with AI not because of AI's limitations, but because of **unclear user instructions**.

Prompting isn't just searching. It's **'programming in natural language.'**

A new form of software development where sentences replace code and language models replace compilers.

### Core Principle: Clarity of Thought

> **I cannot solve what I cannot explain; neither can AI.**

Before asking, first ask yourself:

- What exactly is the result I want?
- What conditions must this result satisfy to be a success?
- What information does AI need to give an accurate answer?

---

## 2. Core Theory: 7 Essential Prompting Techniques

Seven core techniques you can apply depending on the situation to get better results.

### 2.1 Persona: Assigning a Role

**Purpose:** Avoid vague generalities and get answers from a specific perspective with expertise.

| Weak Prompt | Strong Prompt |
| --- | --- |
| "Tell me marketing strategy" | "You are a CMO with 20+ years in B2B SaaS. Given our Series A startup's budget constraints, advise us." |

**Application Tip:** Combining role + experience/expertise + current situation maximizes effect.

---

### 2.2 Context: Providing Background Information

**Purpose:** Prevent AI's guessing and hallucination. Without information, AI has no choice but to make plausible-sounding false statements.

**ABC Principle: Always Be Contexting**

Types of context to provide:

- **Situation Context:** Current business situation, project stage
- **Constraints:** Budget, time, technical limits, team size
- **History:** What you've already tried, failed approaches
- **Audience:** Who will use the result

---

### 2.3 Output Requirements: Specifying Format

**Purpose:** Clarify desired form to minimize revision iterations.

Elements to specify:

- **Format:** Table, list, report, code, JSON, etc.
- **Length:** Number of paragraphs, character count, duration (for presentations)
- **Tone:** Formal/informal, technical/general
- **Structure:** Required sections, content to exclude

**Example:**

```
- Format: 1-page executive summary for management
- Tone: Formal, data-driven
- Structure: Executive summary → 3 recommendations → expected risks
```

---

### 2.4 Few-Shot Learning: Providing Examples

**Purpose:** One good example teaches AI more accurately than a hundred words of explanation.

**Application Method:**

```
Write product descriptions in the following format.

[Example Input] Wireless earbuds, noise canceling, 8-hour battery
[Example Output] "Escape daily noise. Eight hours of continuous playback 
lets you focus on your music all day."

[Actual Input] Foldable keyboard, Bluetooth, multi-pairing 3 devices
[Request] Write in the same style as the example above.
```

---

### 2.5 Chain of Thought: Making Reasoning Explicit

**Purpose:** AI walks through logical steps, improving accuracy on complex problems.

**Application Method:**

- "Think step by step"
- "First analyze the problem, then provide the solution"
- "Analyze pros and cons of each option before concluding"

**Effective for:** Math problems, logical reasoning, complex decisions, code debugging

---

### 2.6 Tree of Thoughts: Exploring Multiple Paths

**Purpose:** Avoid getting stuck on single solutions; compare alternatives for optimal answer.

**Application Method:**

```
Present 3 different approaches to this problem,
analyze pros, cons, and applicable conditions for each,
then select your final recommendation.
```

**Effective for:** Strategy development, technical architecture design, decisions with complex tradeoffs

---

### 2.7 Adversarial Collaboration: Introducing Critique

**Purpose:** Self-validation improves answer completeness and balance.

**Application Method:**

```
First write the proposal, then from a critical reviewer's perspective,
identify 3 weaknesses, then revise the final version incorporating this critique.
```

**Variation:** "Play devil's advocate and find 3 holes in this plan"

---

## 3. Integrated Example: Applying Techniques Together

Below is a practical prompt example integrating the above techniques.

---

### Example: Creating GTM Strategy for New Service

```
[PERSONA]
You are a Product Marketing Manager with 10+ years at both large IT companies
and startups, with strong experience launching B2B SaaS products domestically.

[CONTEXT]
We are a 50-person HR tech startup.
- Product: AI-powered recruitment interview analysis solution
- Target: HR teams at mid-size companies with 100+ annual hires
- Current Status: MVP complete, 3 pilot customers, preparing official launch
- Constraints: 5 million won monthly marketing budget, 1 dedicated marketer
- Competitors: 1 large conglomerate subsidiary (high price), 2 foreign solutions (no Korean support)

[TASK]
Develop GTM (Go-to-Market) strategy to acquire 10 paying customers in 3 months.

[OUTPUT REQUIREMENTS]
- Length: Within 2 pages
- Structure: (1) Strategy summary (2) Monthly execution plan (3) KPIs 
           (4) Expected risks and response
- Tone: Concrete, immediately actionable by practitioners

[ADDITIONAL INSTRUCTIONS]
1. First define our product's competitive advantage and target customer's core Pain Point.
2. Compare at least 2 different GTM approaches, then select final strategy.
3. After initial draft, conduct critical review from CFO perspective on ROI and resource 
   efficiency, then finalize incorporating feedback.
```

## 4. Core Summary

| Technique | Key Question | Apply When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Persona | "Whose perspective do I need?" | Expertise required |
| Context | "What does AI not know?" | Always |
| Output Requirements | "What should the final result look like?" | Always |
| Few-Shot | "Is this a pattern hard to explain in words?" | Style/format critical |
| Chain of Thought | "Does this need complex reasoning?" | Logical problem-solving |
| Tree of Thoughts | "Do I need to compare alternatives?" | Strategic decisions |
| Adversarial Collaboration | "Does this need validation?" | Writing important results |

---

Good prompts start with good questions. If you can't explain something clearly to AI, it's because you haven't thought it through sufficiently.

Hello! We live in a digital world, and you've probably heard that computers use a language made up of '0' and '1', which is called **binary**. This number system differs from the **decimal** we use in our daily lives and can sometimes feel unfamiliar and difficult.

But don't worry! Today, we'll learn very **easy and simple methods to convert decimal to binary and binary to decimal**. Like learning a different language, experience the fun of navigating the world of numbers!

### Why do we need to know conversion methods?

- **Understanding computer language:** Essential when studying programming or computer architecture.
- **Grasping digital principles:** Helps understand how digital data is represented and stored.
- **Improving problem-solving skills:** Develops logical thinking and mathematical intuition.

So, shall we start our first conversion journey?

### Journey 1: Converting decimal to binary! (Decimal to Binary)

Let's convert the familiar decimal we use into binary, the language of computers. There are two easy methods!

#### Method 1: Divide by 2 and read remainders backwards (most reliable!)

This is the most widely used method and works accurately for any number.

1. **Divide the decimal number by 2.**
2. **Write down the quotient and remainder (0 or 1).**
3. **Continue dividing the quotient by 2 until the quotient becomes 0.** (Keep writing down the remainders!)
4. **Reading the remainders backwards, from last to first**, that's your binary number!

**Example: Converting decimal 13 to binary**

```
13 ÷ 2 = quotient 6, remainder 1
 6 ÷ 2 = quotient 3, remainder 0
 3 ÷ 2 = quotient 1, remainder 1
 1 ÷ 2 = quotient 0, remainder 1  <-- Quotient is 0! Stop!
```

Reading remainders **from bottom to top**? **1101**  
Yes, decimal 13 is **1101** in binary! Easy, right?

#### Method 2: Subtracting powers of 2 (intuitive and fast!)

If you're familiar with powers of 2 (1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32...), this method will feel faster.

1. **Find the largest power of 2 that is less than or equal to the decimal number you want to convert.**
2. **Subtract the found value from the decimal, and mark '1' in the corresponding power position.**
3. **Repeat steps 1 and 2 with the result until you reach 0.**
4. **Fill unused power positions with '0'.**

**Example: Converting decimal 13 to binary**

- (Powers of 2: ..., 16, **8**, **4**, 2, **1**)
- 13 >= **8** ($2^3$) ? Yes! -> 13 - 8 = 5. (Bit position: `1 _ _ _`)
- 5 >= **4** ($2^2$) ? Yes! -> 5 - 4 = 1. (Bit position: `1 1 _ _`)
- 1 >= 2 ($2^1$) ? No! (Bit position: `1 1 0 _`)
- 1 >= **1** ($2^0$) ? Yes! -> 1 - 1 = 0. (Bit position: `1 1 0 1`)

All positions are filled! The result is **1101** again.

### Journey 2: Converting binary to decimal! (Binary to Decimal)

Now let's do the opposite - convert binary, the language of computers, into our familiar decimal. This is even simpler!

#### Core method: Add the place values (powers of 2)!

Each position in a binary number has a value of $2^0(1), 2^1(2), 2^2(4), 2^3(8)...$from right to left. **Just add up all the values where there's a '1'!**

1. **Write the place value (power of 2) above each position of the binary number.** (From right to left: 1, 2, 4, 8...)
2. **Select only the place values where the binary digit is '1'.**
3. **Add all the selected place values together.**

**Example: Converting binary 1101 to decimal**

```
  Place value:   8   4   2   1   (2^3, 2^2, 2^1, 2^0)
  Binary:        1   1   0   1
-----------------------------
  Values where '1': 8,  4,     1
```

Let's add them up? 8 + 4 + 1 = **13**

Yes, binary 1101 is back to decimal 13!

**One more! Example: Converting binary 10110 to decimal**

```
  Place value:  16   8   4   2   1   (2^4 ~ 2^0)
  Binary:        1   0   1   1   0
-----------------------------
  Values where '1': 16,     4,  2
```

Adding them all? 16 + 4 + 2 = **22**  
Binary 10110 is 22 in decimal!

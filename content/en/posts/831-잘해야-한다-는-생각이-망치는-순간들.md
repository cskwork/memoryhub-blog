> Fields Medal mathematician Huh Jun-ho on groundless confidence and the power of simple daily life

# Key Points

- **Power of simplifying daily life**: Reducing stimuli and maintaining repeated routines preserves 'space' for ideas to emerge.
- **15-minute hourglass focus method**: Make time visible, habitually achieve short, clear immersion.
- **Connecting past, present, and future 'self' with kindness**: Today's small kindness saves the future you (Seoul National University graduation speech message).
- **Winding paths become 'optimal routes'**: Trial and error becomes stepping stones in retrospect. Experience takes priority over moment-to-moment 'evaluation.'
- **'I must do well' breaks you**: Replace achievement obsession with curiosity and process focus.
- **Groundless confidence**: Draw strength from self-trust independent of external metrics rather than grades and titles.
- **The value of mathematics (deep study)**: The ability to enjoy difficult challenges (a transferable superpower) + math as humanity's most precise language for preserving and conveying thought.

---

When work stalls, we seek new stimuli to find solutions. But Fields Medal winner Huh Jun-ho made the opposite choice. **Simplify daily life.** Same sleep time, same eating place, same clothes—repetition.

Instead of adding new stimuli, **preserve space for ideas to grow**.

Stimulation brings joy, but sometimes churns the mind's soil and breaks the sprouts.

### 

### 1) Why Simplify Daily Life

Routines are **tools that reduce cognitive friction**. Decision fatigue diminishes, and remaining energy focuses on essential problems. Huh cautiously guards against becoming fixed in exaggerated images. The key is **flexible simplicity**—not restraint for show, but life design that preserves **space** for thought.

### 2) 15-Minute Hourglass: Short, Clear Immersion

His favorite tool is a **15-minute hourglass**. Visualizing time reduces stray thoughts and creates **clear boundaries**—"only this topic until it empties." If long focus is difficult, repeat short immersion frequently.

Small wins create **virtuous cycles that trigger the next immersion**.

### 3) Kindness Connecting Past, Present, Future

At Seoul National University's graduation ceremony, he spoke: **Past me, today's me, distant future me**—strangers to each other, yet each day connects these three. "Be kind to one another, and to yourselves." Today's small kindness becomes **the greatest gift to your future self**. Remember:

**Sometimes we're harsher to ourselves than tired others.**

### 4) Winding Paths Can Be 'Optimal Routes'

Looking back, **countless detours were actually essential steps**. The circumlocution that seemed unnecessary then becomes **the fastest path** once you reach the destination.

That's why what matters isn't the anxious moment-to-moment evaluation of "I must do well," but **experiencing fully**.

### 5) 'I Must Do Well' Breaks You

What freezes us is usually **performance obsession**. "I must do well this time" amplifies fear of failure and kills curiosity. Huh's advice is simple:

**Return to curiosity-centered, process-centered.**

Performance follows.

### 6) The Source of Unshaken Confidence

Confidence based on grades, rankings, titles **crumbles when circumstances change**. Graduate school and research environments have too many "people like me." That's why he speaks of **groundless confidence**—**self-trust independent of external metrics**.

"I can learn, endure, and try again."

This belief **bends but doesn't break** when situations shift.

### 7) What Mathematics Taught

Mathematics (or any deep study) imprints **joy in tackling difficult problems** on your being. This ability transfers across fields—a kind of **superpower**. Simultaneously, mathematics is **humanity's most precise language for recording and passing down thought**.

It has value at both personal and societal levels.

---

## Practical Guide to Apply Now

- **Redesign routines (1 week)**: Arrange wake-sleep-meals-work place toward **simple and repetitive**. Allow only 1 'variation day' weekly for flexibility.
- **4 blocks of 15 minutes**: Flip your hourglass, **one topic per block**. 3-5 minutes complete rest between blocks.
- **Kindness log**: Before bed, record **one small kindness** you showed your future self today.
- **Performance-free zone**: 10 minutes before work, **ban goals, rankings, evaluation**. Write today's one question and begin.
- **Reframe confidence source**: "I'm first place so..." → "I can keep iterating, revising, learning..."

---

## Closing

**Flip a 15-minute hourglass today.** That 15 minutes might be the first clue to your own winding but speediest path.

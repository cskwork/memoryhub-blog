The book **"The Art of Consistency"** (by Shimpachi Inoue) presents core principles for maintaining consistency. I'll explain them in greater detail. The author emphasizes that **consistency** should not rely solely on willpower, but rather on **creating small structures and systems, and automating behaviors**.

### 1. **Make a 'Decision' to Do It Every Day**

- **Core Idea:** The essence of consistency is practicing it **every day**.  
  For example, the author emphasizes that **exercising daily** is easier than exercising 1-2 times per week. Why? Because it eliminates the time spent debating whether to do it or not.  
  This way, behavior can be **automated** without relying on willpower.  
  **Implementation Method:** When organizing your daily schedule, clearly specify "what I'll do today" and decide in advance what you'll accomplish.

### 2. **Keep Going Small Rather Than Perfect**

- **Core Idea:** Rather than striving for perfection from the start, prioritize **small, consistent execution**.  
  For example, if you want to read a book, start with **one page per day**.  
  This is a method of starting with the **minimum unit** and gradually expanding from there.  
  **Implementation Method:** Initially set very small goals like "one page per day," and execute them consistently. Gradually increase your targets.

### 3. **Attach a 'Small Preliminary Action'**

- **Core Idea:** Before starting a new behavior, **add a small action as a prerequisite** to make it habitual.  
  For example, before reading a book, **brew coffee or boil water first**.  
  By attaching this small preliminary action, you can link it like a kind of 'conditioned reflex.'  
  **Implementation Method:** For example, connect the habit by always opening your book and starting to read before having your coffee.

### 4. **Make 'When' Concrete**

- **Core Idea:** The phrase "I'll do it when I have time" provides **flexibility** but **lacks specificity in execution**.  
  Instead, setting a **specific time** is important.  
  For example, set a specific time like **"Exercise at 8 AM."**  
  **Implementation Method:** When planning your day, set each activity at a precise **time and location** and execute accordingly.

### 5. **Accelerate with Recording**

- **Core Idea:** By recording and visualizing executed behaviors, **motivation for action** is strengthened.  
  For example, use a **habit tracker** to **record daily practice**.  
  **Implementation Method:** Check off what you did today in a smartphone app or calendar, and visually confirm the continuity of your practice.

---

### **Specific Methods and Tools**

- **Set/Chaining:** A method of **linking** a new habit to an existing habit. For example, if you **start reading immediately after washing your face**, you can naturally continue the new habit.
- **Micro Morning Routine:** Create a **tiny routine you can do daily** to **reduce friction** in starting your day. For example, create a very simple habit like **opening the window and breathing fresh air for 10 seconds right after waking up**.
- **Identity Shift:** Redefine your goal not as someone **"who does things well"** but as someone **"who does things every day."** Rather than achieving the goal, you **identify yourself** as someone who practices consistently. This approach can make consistent behavior easier.

---

### **7-Day Mini Application Plan**

The author presents a **practical 7-day plan for consistency**.

| **Stage** | **Execution Plan** |
| --- | --- |
| **1. Redefine Goal** | Set your goal action-focused rather than outcome-focused. Example: "Read one page per day" |
| **2. Set Trigger** | Set a **trigger** to perform a specific action. Example: "Start reading after morning wash" |
| **3. Add Preliminary Action** | **Connect actions** by adding a new habit to the previous action. Example: Boil water, then open book |
| **4. Execute for 30+ Seconds** | Execute for at least **30 seconds**, then you can gain additional bonuses. |
| **5. Record in Habit Tracker** | Record daily practice to **visualize** it. |
| **6. Resume If You Miss a Day** | If you miss a day, **start again**. No penalties or rewards. |
| **7. Sunday Review** | Check your goal on the weekend and make **minor adjustments** if needed. |

---

### **One-Line Conclusion**

**Consistency is not talent, it's design.** Start small, gradually expand, and sustain habits through automation.
